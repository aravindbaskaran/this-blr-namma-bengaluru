Why this guide exists

When I go somewhere new, I like living the place for however long I'm there, rather than just visiting it - that's genuinely been one of the more fun habits of my life, and this guide is me sharing that habit for Bengaluru specifically.

India is enormous, and a lot of it goes underrepresented. What gets top billing - in guidebooks, in stock photography, in the default idea of "Indian culture" - is overwhelmingly North Indian. Cross south of the Vindhyas and a lot of what gets marketed as "traditional" starts to feel foreign to what's actually around you. This is an attempt to reclaim some of that representation: the cultural and regional diversity Bengaluru itself holds, inside a country that so often flattens it to one picture.

I'll admit "avoid the cliche" is a governing instinct of mine - though I'm aware that saying so is itself a bit of a cliche. Consider this guide my one honest attempt at it anyway.

So: this is a guide to this Bengaluru of ours, in the order you'd actually want it - history first, then what you could do here (walk, sit, eat, drink, try, buy), then what's actually traditional here (spoiler: not kurtas and lehengas), and then everything Bengaluru sits inside of - the rest of Karnataka.

ಇತಿಹಾಸa short history

A garden and lake city, long before it was a tech city

Bengaluru is nearly 500 years old. The software industry accounts for maybe the last 15 of those.

Ground older than the city, older than most continents

Before any of the dates below, there's the land itself. Bengaluru sits on the Peninsular Gneiss - among the oldest exposed rock on Earth, dated to 3-3.4 billion years, older than the Himalayas, older than dinosaurs, older than atmospheric oxygen. The best exposures are at Lalbagh and Basavanagudi's Bugle Rock, both declared National Geological Monuments in 1975. This bedrock is part of the Dharwar Craton, a fragment of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana that once also included Antarctica, Africa and Australia, before continental drift pulled them apart. The city's undulating, hilly terrain and its elevation - over 900m, with no major river nearby - is why it grew into a "city of a thousand lakes": rainwater was dammed into cascading tanks down three natural valleys, over 2,300 of them by 1897. Fewer than 200 survive today. The terrain is still there.

Why Bengaluru, not Bangalore

On 1 November 2014 - Karnataka Rajyotsava, the state's formation day - the Karnataka government officially renamed Bengaluru and eleven other cities, restoring their Kannada names after decades of anglicised spellings from the colonial era. The proposal had been pending since 2005, first raised by the writer U. R. Ananthamurthy. It's the same reasoning behind Mysuru, Mangaluru, Belagavi, Vijayapura, Hubballi and Chikkamagaluru elsewhere on this page - a correction, on the same footing as Bombay becoming Mumbai or Madras becoming Chennai.

1537

Kempe Gowda I lays out the first pete

A mud fort and a market town at what's now City Market and Chickpet, with tanks dug alongside it - Dharmambudhi, Kempambudhi, Sampangi - that gave the young city its first lakes.

1760s

Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan rebuild and expand

The fort is rebuilt in stone under Mysore rule, and Lalbagh is laid out as a royal garden - part of why "garden city" predates any British influence.

1809

The British Cantonment splits the city in two

A colonial garrison and civil lines rise east of the old pete - MG Road, Richmond Town, Ulsoor, Fraser Town - creating a twin-city pattern that still shapes how the city reads today.

1940s-60s

"Pensioners' Paradise" and the PSU boom

HAL, ITI, BEL and HMT plant Bengaluru as India's aerospace and precision-manufacturing hub. A mild climate and mature parks earn it a genteel, unhurried reputation.

1980s-90s

Electronics City and the "Silicon Valley of India"

Infosys sets up south of the city in the early '80s. By the mid-90s, the nickname sticks, and talent and capital start pulling east and south of the old core.

2000s

Whitefield and the Outer Ring Road corridor

ITPL and the ring road belt - Marathahalli, Bellandur, Sarjapur, HSR - become the new center of gravity, often built over the lakes that once irrigated paddy fields.

2008-now

A new airport pulls growth north

Kempegowda International Airport opens, and Devanahalli, Yelahanka and Hebbal start growing to meet it - even as Basavanagudi, Malleswaram, Jayanagar and Rajajinagar stay the city's most walkable, rooted ground.

ಇದು, ಅದಲ್ಲthis, not that

What "India" usually means on screen, and what's also true here

None of this is a correction - the north is real too. It's just not the whole map, and Bengaluru sits south of the Vindhyas, on the other side of that line.

~1,750 km south of Delhi as the crow flies (over 2,100 km by road)

Further still from the Delhi-Agra-Jaipur "Golden Triangle" that supplies most default "India" imagery on screen - even India's own "Incredible India" campaigns leaned on Rajasthan and the Taj Mahal as the one signifier that "needs no additional context." Kerala's backwaters get an occasional look-in; Karnataka rarely does.

Everything on this list lives that far south, or further, a day's drive from Bengaluru at most.

ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲಿಯಿರಿlearn a little Kannada

Words that go further than any guidebook

Kannada is Karnataka's official language and Bengaluru's mother tongue, even if English and Hindi both get by. It's also one of only a handful of languages in India with classical-language status - the oldest surviving Kannada inscription is from 450 CE, and Kavirajamarga, the oldest surviving work of Kannada poetics, dates to 850 CE. That's a longer running conversation than most languages on earth get to have, and it's genuinely fun to pick up a few threads of it. Try a few of these in an auto, a darshini, or a market stall - people notice, and it usually gets you a warmer price or a warmer welcome. Tap the speaker icon to hear a phrase - your browser needs a Kannada voice installed for it to sound right, so treat it as a rough guide, not a lesson.

Karnataka: locations, food, and festivals

Bengaluru is one weave in a bigger loom - the regions, dishes, and festivals that surround it, all in one place.

Locations

Mysuru

ಮೈಸೂರುMy-sooru

Wodeyar palaces, Mysore silk shot through with gold zari, sandalwood and Dasara's elephant processions.

Sweet - Mysore Pak
Spicy - Mysuru masala dosa
Cold - Nannari sherbet
Hot - Mysuru saaru
Wear - The Mysuru peta, a pleated silk-and-zari turban worn by the Wodeyars and still given to grooms and honoured guests today.
Watch - Mysore Mallige (1992), a National Award-winning film based on the poet K. S. Narasimhaswamy's collection of the same name.

Udupi & Mangaluru

ಉಡುಪಿ - ಮಂಗಳೂರುUdupi - Mangaluru

Kasavu-bordered cottons, red-tiled roofs, Yakshagana masks, and a coastline running on neer dosa and kori rotti.

Sweet - Mangalore buns
Spicy - Kori rotti
Cold - Kokum sherbet
Hot - Kashaya
Wear - Everyday, a simple lungi and shirt; on stage, the towering headgear and painted faces of Yakshagana, coastal Karnataka's own dance-drama.
Watch - Su From So (2025), a coastal-village comedy-horror shot around Someshwara that became the 7th highest-grossing Kannada film ever - proof the coast still sets the trends.

Hubballi-Dharwad-Belagavi

ಹುಬ್ಬಳ್ಳಿ-ಧಾರವಾಡ-ಬೆಳಗಾವಿHubballi-Dhaarwaad-Belagaavi

Kasuti embroidery worked in black cotton, Ilkal sarees with their temple-tower pallu, jowar rotti country.

Sweet - Dharwad peda & Belagavi kunda
Spicy - Yenne badnekai
Cold - Majjige
Hot - Jolada rotti
Wear - A white pagadi (turban) with a dhoti and long coat - Lingayat formalwear that predates the Mysuru peta by centuries in this belt.
Watch - Nanna Ninna Prema Kathe (2016), shot across Jamakhandi, Bijapur, Bagalkot and Hubballi, with dialogue in true North Karnataka style.

Hampi & Vijayapura

ಹಂಪಿ - ವಿಜಯಪುರHampi - Vijayapura

Vijayanagara boulder-ruins and Deccan sultanate domes - two very different empires, one dry northern landscape.

Sweet - Kesari bath
Spicy - Shenga chutney pudi
Cold - Nimbu sherbet
Hot - Roadside chaha
Wear - The angarkha and pagadi of the Deccan sultanate courts around Vijayapura - a different silhouette from anything worn further south.
Watch - James (2022), a Puneeth Rajkumar blockbuster with a schedule shot on location in Hampi and Kamalapura.

Chikmagalur

ಚಿಕ್ಕಮಗಳೂರುChikka-magaluru

Where Indian coffee began - Baba Budan planted the first beans on these hills in the 1670s, and Mullayanagiri, Karnataka's highest peak, rises right beside them.

Sweet - Coffee halwa
Spicy - Malnad-style chicken curry
Cold - Estate cold coffee
Hot - Filter kaapi, at its source
Wear - A simpler, practical version of the panche-angi suited to plantation work and the cooler hill climate.

Kodagu

ಕೊಡಗುKodagu

The Kodava homeland - misty coffee estates layered over a distinct language, a martial tradition, and a cuisine found nowhere else in Karnataka.

Sweet - Coorg orange
Spicy - Pandi curry
Cold - Estate cold coffee
Hot - Kadambuttu
Wear - The Kodava kupya chele - a black knee-length coat, a gold-embroidered red sash, and a silver peeche kathi dagger tucked into it.
Watch - Mungaru Male (2006), the biggest Kannada blockbuster of its decade, shot largely across Coorg's coffee country.

Bengaluru

ಬೆಂಗಳೂರುBengaluru

Where all of the above land in one city - in its markets, its emporiums, and its Kannadiga kitchens.

Sweet - Iyengar bakery benne biscuit
Spicy - Donne biryani
Cold - Badam milk
Hot - Filter kaapi
Wear - The panche and angi (dhoti and kurta) for festivals and weddings, often finished with a Mysuru peta borrowed from the old capital.
Watch - U-Turn (2016), a Bengaluru-set thriller built around a real urban legend about a city flyover.

About the person behind this

I was born in Pondicherry, and by the time I got to Bengaluru I'd already been through seven schools and two colleges spread across Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and Kolkata. I've moved around so much that I don't really belong anywhere anymore - which, funnily enough, means I belong everywhere a little bit too, and it's probably where the habit on this page's opening comes from.

Bengaluru is as representative of India as anywhere else - but it holds far more than the software-and-startups image most people carry in from outside. As an outsider who's chosen to stay, I've come to think the city's older identity, the one built over centuries and not just the last two decades, deserves a guide of its own.

There's also a narrower reason this page cites its sources. A lot of what gets said about Bengaluru - in listicles, reels, "hidden gem" posts - quietly overrides the work and context that came before it, without crediting where any of it came from. This page isn't out to relitigate anyone's claim to the city. It keeps the reference-worthy details - the founding date, the lake names, the source of a dish or a craft - written down with a source attached, so they don't have to be rediscovered from scratch every few years.

India is not one story.
Bengaluru isn't either.

Most guides to "India" default to the north - the Taj, the sitar, Bollywood, garlic naan and paneer. This one starts from Bengaluru: a garden city of jacaranda trees, filter kaapi, five languages within a day's drive, and a few thousand years of temple-building next door to a few decades of tech campuses.

ಈ ಮಾರ್ಗದರ್ಶಿ ಬಗ್ಗೆabout this guide

Why this guide exists

When I go somewhere new, I like living the place for however long I'm there, rather than just visiting it - that's genuinely been one of the more fun habits of my life, and this guide is me sharing that habit for Bengaluru specifically.

India is enormous, and a lot of it goes underrepresented. What gets top billing - in guidebooks, in stock photography, in the default idea of "Indian culture" - is overwhelmingly North Indian. Cross south of the Vindhyas and a lot of what gets marketed as "traditional" starts to feel foreign to what's actually around you. This is an attempt to reclaim some of that representation: the cultural and regional diversity Bengaluru itself holds, inside a country that so often flattens it to one picture.

I'll admit "avoid the cliche" is a governing instinct of mine - though I'm aware that saying so is itself a bit of a cliche. Consider this guide my one honest attempt at it anyway.

So: this is a guide to this Bengaluru of ours, in the order you'd actually want it - history first, then what you could do here (walk, sit, eat, drink, try, buy), then what's actually traditional here (draped cloth and local tailoring - not only the north-Indian festive default), and then everything Bengaluru sits inside of - the rest of Karnataka.

ಇತಿಹಾಸa short history

A garden and lake city, long before it was a tech city

Bengaluru is nearly 500 years old as a founded city. The software industry accounts for maybe the last 35–40 of those — KEONICS laid out Electronic City in 1978, Infosys moved its headquarters here in 1983, and Texas Instruments opened an R&D centre in 1985.

Ground older than the city, older than most continents

Before any of the dates below, there's the land itself. Bengaluru sits on the Peninsular Gneiss - among the oldest exposed rock on Earth, dated to 3-3.4 billion years, older than the Himalayas, older than dinosaurs, older than atmospheric oxygen. The best exposures are at Lalbagh and Basavanagudi's Bugle Rock, both declared National Geological Monuments in 1975. This bedrock is part of the Dharwar Craton, a fragment of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana that once also included Antarctica, Africa and Australia, before continental drift pulled them apart. The city's undulating, hilly terrain and its elevation - over 900m, with no major river nearby - is why it grew into a "city of a thousand lakes": rainwater was dammed into cascading tanks down three natural valleys, over 2,300 of them by 1897. Fewer than 200 survive today. Tank irrigation on this plateau is older than the pete: Western Ganga and Chola-era keris already held water here — the 890 CE Begur inscription is one surviving witness — so Kempe Gowda was extending a water culture, not inventing it. The terrain is still there.

Why Bengaluru, not Bangalore

On 1 November 2014 - Karnataka Rajyotsava, the state's formation day - the Karnataka government officially renamed Bengaluru and eleven other cities, restoring their Kannada names after decades of anglicised spellings from the colonial era. The proposal had been pending since 2005, first raised by the writer U. R. Ananthamurthy. It's the same reasoning behind Mysuru, Mangaluru, Belagavi, Vijayapura, Hubballi and Chikkamagaluru elsewhere on this page - a correction, on the same footing as Bombay becoming Mumbai or Madras becoming Chennai.

ಇದು, ಅದಲ್ಲthis, not that

What "India" usually means on screen, and what's also true here

None of this is a correction - the north is real too. It's just not the whole map, and Bengaluru sits south of the Vindhyas, on the other side of that line.

~1,750 km south of Delhi as the crow flies (over 2,100 km by road)

Further still from the Delhi-Agra-Jaipur "Golden Triangle" that supplies most default "India" imagery on screen - even India's own "Incredible India" campaigns leaned on Rajasthan and the Taj Mahal as the one signifier that "needs no additional context." Kerala's backwaters get an occasional look-in; Karnataka rarely does.

Everything on this list lives that far south, or further, a day's drive from Bengaluru at most.

ಇಂದಿನ ಬೆಂಗಳೂರುtoday's Bengaluru

If you're here for work, coffee and city

Everything above is real Bengaluru too, but if you've flown in for a working week rather than a heritage tour, this is the layer you actually need.

Work

You don't need to base yourself near a business district for any of this - all of it is spread across the city, so pick by neighbourhood, not by proximity to an office park.

BHIVE

Bengaluru's own coworking brand, not an import - homegrown, community-first, with locations across the city so you're never far from one. Day passes are easy; you don't need a membership to just show up and work.

The wider coworking scene

91springboard, WeWork, IndiQube and others all have a real presence here too, each with its own crowd and price point. Most take day passes - worth trying a couple before committing to one for a longer stay.

The meetup calendar

Bengaluru's tech and founder meetup scene runs continuously, not just around big conferences - check Meetup or Luma for what's on during your actual dates. Showing up to one is often the fastest way into the city's working life.

Coffee

Not third-wave-coffee-abroad in a different postcode - these are genuinely Bengaluru's own, worth knowing by name.

Hatti Kaapi

A Bengaluru-born, stand-and-go filter coffee chain - outlets across the city and at the airport, so it's often the first and last coffee of a trip. Try the Bella Kaapi, filter coffee sweetened with jaggery instead of sugar. The brand is also notable for employing specially-abled staff and senior citizens at many outlets.

Blue Tokai

A specialty roaster with real depth in the city, especially around Indiranagar - closer to a sit-down work session than a quick cup, with proper single-origin pour-overs if that's what you're after.

Katte Kulture

A Jayanagar café in a restored heritage house, founded by two sisters and inspired by the coffee estates of Chikmagalur - garden seating, farm-to-table coffee, millet-based snacks. Named for the katte, the stone platform outside old Bengaluru homes where neighbours gather to talk.

Alchemy Coffee Roasters

Also Jayanagar, and a direct thread back to Chikmagalur - single-origin beans from the Baarbara Estate, roasted in-house, in a brick-and-wood space that reads more coffee-nerd than photo-op.

Calm Coffee

Also in Jayanagar - a striking, deliberately minimal building carved from deep earth tones, built to feel like a pause from the street outside. Worth a visit for the architecture as much as the coffee.

City

Bengaluru's mild elevation and morning weather make outdoor exercise genuinely pleasant most of the year - go early, before both the heat and the traffic build up.

Cubbon Park

Closed to traffic 5:30-8am daily, so it's one of the few places in the city you can run or cycle on open road. A regular crowd of walkers and joggers who mostly know each other by face.

Lalbagh

Same early-morning window, more shade and hills - best before 8am, when it belongs to walkers and mynas rather than tour groups.

Sankey Tank

A paved lakeside loop in Malleswaram with an open gym alongside it - shorter than Cubbon or Lalbagh, but a reliable daily option if you're staying nearby.

Kanteerava Stadium

An actual 400m public running track, resurfaced in 2024, if you want to run intervals rather than just loop a park.

Nandi Hills, for cyclists

The city's classic weekend hill climb - about 60km out, best started well before sunrise to beat both the heat and the return traffic. Turahalli forest, off Kanakapura Road, is the shorter, closer alternative for off-road trail riding.

ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲಿಯಿರಿlearn Kannada

Words that go further than any guidebook

Kannada is Karnataka's official language and Bengaluru's mother tongue - you can get away with some English and some Hindi, but the city itself runs on Kannada first, Tamil to a real extent, and a scatter of other languages after that. It's one of eleven languages in India with classical-language status - a list that also includes Tamil, Sanskrit, Telugu, Malayalam, Odia, Marathi, Bengali, Assamese, Pali, and Prakrit. The oldest surviving Kannada inscription is from 450 CE, and Kavirajamarga, the oldest surviving work of Kannada poetics, dates to 850 CE. That's a longer running conversation than most languages on earth get to have, and it's genuinely fun to pick up a few threads of it. Try a few of these in an auto, a darshini, or a market stall - people notice. Kannada, like most Indian languages, rewards the effort: a warmer smile, a warmer welcome, and sometimes even a warmer price. Tap the speaker icon to hear a phrase - your browser needs a Kannada voice installed for it to sound right, so treat it as a rough guide, not a lesson.

Karnataka: locations, food, and festivals

Bengaluru is one weave in a bigger loom - the regions, dishes, and festivals that surround it, all in one place.

Locations

Mysuru

ಮೈಸೂರುMy-sooru

Wodeyar palaces, Mysore silk shot through with gold zari, sandalwood and Dasara's elephant processions.

Sweet - Mysore Pak
Spicy - Mysuru masala dosa
Cold - Nannari sherbet
Hot - Mysuru saaru
Watch - Mysore Mallige (1992), a National Award-winning film based on the poet K. S. Narasimhaswamy's collection of the same name.

Udupi & Mangaluru

ಉಡುಪಿ - ಮಂಗಳೂರುUdupi - Mangaluru

Kasavu-bordered cottons, red-tiled roofs, Yakshagana masks, and a coastline running on neer dosa and kori rotti.

Sweet - Mangalore buns
Spicy - Kori rotti
Cold - Kokum sherbet
Hot - Kashaya
Watch - Su From So (2025), a coastal-village horror-comedy shot around Someshwara - now the 4th highest-grossing Kannada film of all time, behind only the two KGF films and Kantara.

Hubballi-Dharwad-Belagavi

ಹುಬ್ಬಳ್ಳಿ-ಧಾರವಾಡ-ಬೆಳಗಾವಿHubballi-Dhaarwaad-Belagaavi

Kasuti embroidery worked in black cotton, Ilkal sarees with their temple-tower pallu, jowar rotti country.

Sweet - Dharwad peda & Belagavi kunda
Spicy - Yenne badnekai
Cold - Majjige
Hot - Jolada rotti
Watch - Nanna Ninna Prema Kathe (2016), shot across Jamakhandi, Bijapur, Bagalkot and Hubballi, with dialogue in true North Karnataka style.

Hampi & Vijayapura

ಹಂಪಿ - ವಿಜಯಪುರHampi - Vijayapura

Vijayanagara boulder-ruins and Deccan sultanate domes - two very different empires, one dry northern landscape.

Sweet - Kesari bath
Spicy - Shenga chutney pudi
Cold - Nimbu sherbet
Hot - Roadside chaha
Watch - James (2022), a Puneeth Rajkumar blockbuster with a schedule shot on location in Hampi and Kamalapura.

Chikmagalur

ಚಿಕ್ಕಮಗಳೂರುChikka-magaluru

Where Indian coffee began - Baba Budan planted the first beans on these hills in the 1670s, and Mullayanagiri, Karnataka's highest peak, rises right beside them.

Sweet - Coffee halwa
Spicy - Malnad-style chicken curry
Cold - Estate cold coffee
Hot - Filter kaapi, at its source
Watch - Love Mocktail (2020), one of the biggest Kannada hits of its year, shot partly at Samse's tea estates - it's since become a three-film series.

Kodagu

ಕೊಡಗುKodagu

The Kodava homeland - misty coffee estates, the black-and-gold kupya with its red chele sash, a martial tradition, and a cuisine found nowhere else in Karnataka.

Sweet - Coorg orange
Spicy - Pandi curry
Cold - Estate cold coffee
Hot - Kadambuttu
Watch - Mungaru Male (2006), shot largely around Coorg and Jog Falls - the first Kannada film to cross ₹50 crore, and still the longest-running film at any Indian multiplex.

Bengaluru

ಬೆಂಗಳೂರುBengaluru

Where all of the above land in one city - in its markets, its emporiums, and its Kannadiga kitchens.

Sweet - Iyengar bakery benne biscuit
Spicy - Donne biryani
Cold - Badam milk
Hot - Filter kaapi
Watch - U-Turn (2016), a Bengaluru-set supernatural thriller - director Pawan Kumar wrote it after his own frustration with a real flyover near his commute.

India is not one story.
Bengaluru isn't either.

Most guides to "India" default to the north - the Taj, the sitar, Bollywood, wheat and paneer. This one starts from Bengaluru: a garden city of jacaranda trees, filter kaapi, five languages within a day's drive, and a few thousand years of temple-building next door to a few decades of tech campuses.

ಇತಿಹಾಸa short history

A garden and lake city, long before it was a tech city

Bengaluru is nearly 500 years old. The software industry accounts for maybe the last 15 of those - worth knowing before you decide which parts of the city are "the real one."

Ground older than the city, older than most continents

Before any of the dates below, there's the land itself. Bengaluru sits on the Peninsular Gneiss - among the oldest exposed rock on Earth, dated to 3-3.4 billion years, older than the Himalayas, older than dinosaurs, older than atmospheric oxygen. The best exposures are at Lalbagh and Basavanagudi's Bugle Rock, both declared National Geological Monuments in 1975. This bedrock is part of the Dharwar Craton, a fragment of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana that once also included Antarctica, Africa and Australia, before continental drift pulled them apart. The city's undulating, hilly terrain and its elevation - over 900m, with no major river nearby - is exactly why it grew into a "city of a thousand lakes": rainwater was dammed into cascading tanks down three natural valleys, over 2,300 of them by 1897. Fewer than 200 survive today, but the terrain that made them possible hasn't gone anywhere.

Why Bengaluru, not Bangalore

On 1 November 2014 - Karnataka Rajyotsava, the state's formation day - the Karnataka government officially renamed Bengaluru and eleven other cities, restoring their Kannada names after decades of anglicised spellings from the colonial era. The proposal had been pending since 2005, first raised by the writer U. R. Ananthamurthy. It's the same reasoning behind Mysuru, Mangaluru, Belagavi, Vijayapura, Hubballi and Chikkamagaluru elsewhere on this page - not a rebrand, but a correction, on the same footing as Bombay becoming Mumbai or Madras becoming Chennai.

1537

Kempe Gowda I lays out the first pete

A mud fort and a market town at what's now City Market and Chickpet, with tanks dug alongside it - Dharmambudhi, Kempambudhi, Sampangi - that gave the young city its first lakes.

1760s

Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan rebuild and expand

The fort is rebuilt in stone under Mysore rule, and Lalbagh is laid out as a royal garden - part of why "garden city" predates any British influence.

1809

The British Cantonment splits the city in two

A colonial garrison and civil lines rise east of the old pete - MG Road, Richmond Town, Ulsoor, Fraser Town - creating a twin-city pattern that still shapes how the city reads today.

1940s-60s

"Pensioners' Paradise" and the PSU boom

HAL, ITI, BEL and HMT plant Bengaluru as India's aerospace and precision-manufacturing hub. A mild climate and mature parks earn it a genteel, unhurried reputation.

1980s-90s

Electronics City and the "Silicon Valley of India"

Infosys sets up south of the city in the early '80s. By the mid-90s, the nickname sticks, and talent and capital start pulling east and south of the old core.

2000s

Whitefield and the Outer Ring Road corridor

ITPL and the ring road belt - Marathahalli, Bellandur, Sarjapur, HSR - become the new center of gravity, often built over the lakes that once irrigated paddy fields.

2008-now

A new airport pulls growth north

Kempegowda International Airport opens, and Devanahalli, Yelahanka and Hebbal start growing to meet it - even as Basavanagudi, Malleswaram, Jayanagar and Rajajinagar stay the city's most walkable, rooted ground.

ಇದು, ಅದಲ್ಲthis, not that

What "India" usually means on screen, and what's also true here

None of this is a correction - the north is real too. It's just not the whole map, and Bengaluru sits south of the Vindhyas, on the other side of that line.

~1,750 km south of Delhi as the crow flies (over 2,100 km by road)

Further still from the Delhi-Agra-Jaipur "Golden Triangle" that supplies most default "India" imagery on screen - even India's own "Incredible India" campaigns leaned on Rajasthan and the Taj Mahal as the one signifier that "needs no additional context." Kerala's backwaters get an occasional look-in; Karnataka rarely does.

Everything on this list lives that far south, or further, a day's drive from Bengaluru at most.

What this is, and how to make it yours

ಬರೆದವರ ಬಗ್ಗೆabout the person behind this

I was born in Pondicherry, and by the time I got to Bengaluru I'd already been through seven schools and two colleges spread across Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and Kolkata. Somewhere in that shuffle, one thing became obvious: the best way to actually enjoy a place is to live it like a local, not visit it like a checklist. I've moved around so much that I don't really belong anywhere anymore - which, funnily enough, means I belong everywhere a little bit too.

Bengaluru is as representative of India as anywhere else - but it holds far more than the software-and-startups image most people carry in from outside. As an outsider who's chosen to stay, I've come to think the city's older identity, the one built over centuries and not just the last two decades, deserves a guide of its own.

There's also a narrower reason this page cites its sources. A lot of what gets said about Bengaluru - in listicles, reels, "hidden gem" posts - quietly overrides the work and context that came before it, without crediting where any of it actually came from. This page isn't trying to correct that or relitigate anyone's claim to the city. It's just trying to keep the reference-worthy details - the founding date, the lake names, the source of a dish or a craft - written down with a source attached, so they don't have to be rediscovered from scratch every few years.

Why it exists

Visitor guides to events happening in Bengaluru often ship with generic "India" branding - Rajasthani motifs, Hindi greetings, north Indian food - none of which is local to this city or to anywhere south of the Vindhyas. This guide starts from what's actually around you: Kannada, Karnataka's own cuisine and crafts, and the neighbourhoods a short auto ride from wherever you're staying.
